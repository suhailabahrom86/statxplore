Lesson 1.3 · Concept

Measures of Central Tendency

Mean, median and mode are measures used to describe the centre or typical position of data.

Mean

Arithmetic average.

Median

Middle observation after ordering.

Mode

Most frequently occurring value.

A calculation is not the final stage. You should explain what the measure tells you about the data.
Lesson 1.3 · Interactive Exploration

Skewness of a Distribution

Skewness describes the direction and degree of asymmetry in a distribution. Move the slider or choose a preset to see how the mean, median and mode change as the tail moves left or right.

← Tail to the leftTail to the right →

Symmetric Distribution

Mean = Median = Mode

Both sides have a similar shape, so all three measures of central tendency are located at approximately the same position.

Example: an approximately normal measurement such as repeated measurement error under stable conditions.

Negative / Left Skew

Longer tail on the left.

Mean < Median < Mode

Symmetric

Both tails are approximately balanced.

Mean = Median = Mode

Positive / Right Skew

Longer tail on the right.

Mode < Median < Mean

Key idea: the mean is pulled most strongly towards the long tail, while the median moves less and the mode remains near the peak.

Concept & Formula Reference

Variance and Standard Deviation for Frequency Data

Before using the formula, understand what the two measures tell us about the spread of a dataset.

Variance

Variance measures how far the observations are spread around the mean by using squared deviations.

A small variance means the values are concentrated close to the mean. A large variance means the values are more widely dispersed.

Measured in squared units

Standard Deviation

Standard deviation is the positive square root of the variance. It describes the spread in the same unit as the original data.

It is therefore usually easier to interpret than variance because it represents a typical distance of observations from the mean.

Measured in the original unit

For frequency or grouped data

Use \(x\) for the observed value. For grouped data, use the class midpoint as \(x\), and use \(f\) for the frequency.

The total number of observations is \(\sum f\). You will need the three totals \(\sum f\), \(\sum fx\), and \(\sum fx^2\).

Formulae

Sample Variance

$$s^2 = \frac{\sum f x^2 - \frac{(\sum f x)^2}{\sum f}}{\sum f - 1}$$

Use this when the observed data are treated as a sample from a larger population.

Sample Standard Deviation

$$s = \sqrt{s^2}$$

After finding the variance, take its positive square root to return to the original measurement unit.

\(f\)Frequency of a value or class.
\(x\)Observed value or class midpoint.
\(\sum f\)Total number of observations.
\(\sum fx\)Sum of frequency × value.
\(\sum fx^2\)Sum of frequency × squared value.
\(s^2, s\)Sample variance and sample standard deviation.

Calculation workflow

1

Prepare the table

Identify \(x\), \(f\), then calculate \(fx\) and \(fx^2\).

2

Find the totals

Calculate \(\sum f\), \(\sum fx\), and \(\sum fx^2\).

3

Calculate variance

Substitute the totals into the appropriate sample or population variance formula.

4

Calculate SD

Take the positive square root of the variance and interpret it in the original unit.

Quick interpretation
A small standard deviation means most observations lie relatively close to the mean, so the dataset is more consistent or tightly clustered.
Important: Variance cannot be negative, and standard deviation is always the positive square root of variance. The next worked examples use these formulae step by step.

Use this:
$$s^2=\frac{\sum fx^2-\frac{(\sum fx)^2}{\sum f}}{\sum f-1}$$
Why not divide by \(n\)?

Step 3: Substitute the household totals

$$s^2=\frac{166-\frac{80^2}{60}}{60-1}$$

$$s=\sqrt{122.6256}\approx11.0736\text{ minutes}$$

Formula & Procedure

Percentiles for Ungrouped Data

A percentile indicates the value below which a given percentage of observations lies. Always arrange the data first.

$$L=\left(\frac{k}{100}\right)n$$

If \(L\) is not an integer

Round \(L\) up to the next whole-number position and use that observation.

If \(L\) is an integer

Use the average of the observations at positions \(L\) and \(L+1\).

Quartiles as percentiles

\(Q_1=P_{25}\), \(Q_2=P_{50}\), and \(Q_3=P_{75}\).

Measures of Central Tendency

Mean, median and mode.

Population Mean
$$ \mu = \frac{\sum x}{N} $$
Sample Mean
$$ \bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n} $$
Mean for Frequency Data
$$ \bar{x} = \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f} $$
Median Position · Odd \(n\)
$$ \text{Median position} = \frac{n+1}{2} $$
Median · Even \(n\)

Variance & Standard Deviation

Measures of variation for population, sample and frequency data.

Population Variance
$$ \sigma^2 = \frac{\sum(x-\mu)^2}{N} $$
Sample Variance
$$ s^2 = \frac{\sum(x-\bar{x})^2}{n-1} $$
Population Standard Deviation
$$ \sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum(x-\mu)^2}{N}} $$
Sample Variance for Frequency Data
$$ s^2 = \frac{\sum fx^2 - \frac{(\sum fx)^2}{\sum f}}{\sum f - 1} $$

Inferential Statistics Using the Same Sample

Inferential statistics moves from the sample to the population of interest. In this project, the same 50 sampled commuting times are used to estimate corresponding population characteristics.

Descriptive Question

What do the 50 sampled students look like?

Answer using \(\bar{x}\), median, mode, \(s^2\), \(s\), quartiles and graphs.

Inferential Question

What might these sample results suggest about the population from which the sample was selected?

Use sample statistics as point estimates of population parameters, while recognising sampling uncertainty.

1. Connect Sample Statistics to Population Parameters

μ

Estimate the Population Mean

Use the sample mean as the point estimate of the population mean.

$$\hat{\mu}=\bar{x}=36.10\text{ min}$$

A suitable report statement is: “The sample mean commuting time is 36.10 minutes; therefore, 36.10 minutes is used as a point estimate of the population mean commuting time.”

σ²

Estimate Population Variability

Use the sample variance and sample standard deviation as estimates of the corresponding population quantities.

$$\hat{\sigma}^2=s^2=148.41\text{ min}^2$$
$$\hat{\sigma}=s=12.18\text{ min}$$

These estimates suggest the population may show a similar level of variation, but the exact population values remain unknown from this sample alone.

2. Write the Sample-to-Population Interpretation

Model interpretation
In the sample of 50 students, the estimated mean commuting time is 36.10 minutes with a sample standard deviation of 12.18 minutes. If the sampling procedure provides a reasonable representation of the population of interest, these sample statistics can be used as estimates of the corresponding population parameters.
Do not overclaim
Do not write “all university students commute for 36 minutes.” The value 36.10 minutes is a sample-based estimate, not an exact population value. A sample can differ from its population because of sampling variation.
